Piezo1: the dome that opens a pore

Two ways to see the same change

Left: the three blades rotate outward as the dome flattens. Right: tension adds a term −γΔA, so the minimum of the free energy slides toward the flat, open state.

What to notice

  1. The membrane is not being pressed. It is bent because the protein's blades are curved, and the bilayer has to follow them.
  2. Flattening increases the channel's in-plane footprint. Under tension, that larger footprint is energetically cheaper, which is the whole engine of the sensor.
  3. The gate only has to widen by about an ångström, while the area change is on the order of 100 nm². That decoupling is why Piezo can be sensitive and still be a narrow, selective pore.

What is settled, what is not

Settled: the closed state is curved and deforms the membrane; tension favours a flatter conformation; the blades are coupled to the pore through the intracellular beams and the cap. Argued: the exact sequence and the degree of flattening in a true open state. High-resolution open structures are hard to obtain, and much of the evidence comes from gain-of-function mutants, simulations, and direct measurements of blade expansion in membranes. Treat the animation as the right cartoon, not as a solved trajectory.
